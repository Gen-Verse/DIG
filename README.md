**Discovery Intelligence Group (DIG)** is a research group at Peking University led by **Ling Yang (杨灵)**. We study how foundation models and agents can move beyond static problem solving toward systems that **learn over long horizons, interact reliably, improve themselves continuously, and participate in open-ended discovery**.

## Research

| Direction | Focus |
|---|---|
| **LLM / Agent Post-Training** | Reasoning, agentic behavior, reward / verifier design, process supervision, long-horizon credit assignment, environment interaction and generalization. |
| **RL Systems / Infrastructure** | Asynchronous training, long-horizon rollouts, multi-environment learning, trajectory management, policy lag, and distributed inference / training. |
| **Recursive Self-Improvement (RSI)** | Experience, memory, skills, data, environment–policy co-evolution, active exploration, and self-evolving harnesses. |
| **AI for Discovery** | Problem discovery and formulation, representation construction, hypothesis formation, intervention and experimentation, evidence-grounded revision, and continual discovery improvement. |

## Open Research

DIG develops open research systems together with **[Gen-Verse](https://github.com/Gen-Verse)**, connecting:

**Research ideas → reusable systems → open-source community**

Selected systems include **ReasonFlux, RLAnything, OpenClaw-RL, GenEnv, MMaDA, dLLM-RL, LatentMAS** and more.
