Building intelligence that can learn, evolve, and discover.

The Discovery Intelligence Group (DIG) studies the learning and systems foundations that move models and agents beyond static problem solving — toward long-horizon training, reliable interaction, recursive self-improvement, and open-ended discovery.

Our thesis

The next leap in AI will not come only from answering harder questions. It will come from systems that can decide what is worth discovering, learn through interaction, revise their own machinery, and improve the process of discovery itself.

DiscoverFind valuable unknowns

Research

Four connected frontiers.

Our research spans algorithms, training infrastructure, agent systems, and discovery-oriented model behavior. Each direction stands on its own while contributing to a common goal: more capable, adaptive, and scientifically useful intelligence.

01

LLM / Agent Post-Training

Reasoning, agentic behavior, reward and verifier design, process supervision, long-horizon credit assignment, environment interaction, and transferable generalization.

Explore direction → 02

RL Systems / Infrastructure

Unified infrastructure for asynchronous RL, long-horizon rollouts, multi-environment learning, trajectory management, policy lag, and distributed inference / training.

Explore direction → 03

Recursive Self-Improvement

Agents that improve through experience, memory, skills, data and environment feedback — including environment–policy co-evolution and self-evolving harnesses.

Explore direction → 04

AI for Discovery

Models that discover and formulate problems, construct and revise representations, form hypotheses, design interventions, and update research processes against external evidence.
